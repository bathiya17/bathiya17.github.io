Research at the intersection of
theoretical physics and computational science

I am an Assistant Professor at McPherson College with a background in theoretical and computational physics. I earned my Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from Wichita State University, where my research focused on Higgs boson production, collider phenomenology, and multi-Higgs processes at future colliders such as muon colliders. My work involves developing Monte Carlo simulation methods for high-energy physics calculations as well as using high-performance and high-throughput computing (HPC/HTC) for large-scale numerical studies. I also hold an M.S. in Physics, where I worked on quantum machine learning and quantum neural networks. My research interests include quantum field theory, particle physics beyond the Standard Model, computational physics, and the application of machine learning techniques to scientific problems in physics and mathematics.
